You walk into the gym, look at the bar and wonder: do I add weight today or not? Double progression answers that for you, and it's one of the safest ways to keep improving for months.

## The idea in two lines

Each exercise has a rep range, say **8 to 12**. First you add **reps** at the same weight. Once you hit the top of the range on every set, you add **weight** and drop back to the bottom of the range.

## An example

Bench press, 3 sets of 8 to 12:

- Week 1: 60 kg × 8, 8, 8
- Week 2: 60 kg × 10, 9, 8
- Week 3: 60 kg × 12, 11, 10
- Week 4: 60 kg × 12, 12, 12 → next time, go up to 62.5 kg
- Week 5: 62.5 kg × 8, 8, 7

Week 5 looks like a step back, but it isn't: you lifted more weight and you've got room to grow your reps again.

## Why it works

- **Small but steady gains.** One extra rep is progress, and it's far easier than adding kilos every week.
- **Technique first.** You only add load once you own the current weight on every set.
- **Fewer injuries.** Weight jumps are rare and small.

## Practical tips

- Use a 6–10 range for heavy lifts (squat, deadlift) and 10–15 for isolation work (curls, lateral raises).
- Make the smallest jump you can: 2.5 kg on the bar, the next dumbbell, or one pin on the machine.
- Leave one or two reps in the tank. Going to failure on every set doesn't speed anything up.
- Log everything. Without a log, you won't know whether you did 10 or 11 last time.
