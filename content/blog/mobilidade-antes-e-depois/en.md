Plenty of people treat warming up and stretching as the same thing: a few minutes of reaching and pulling before training. They're not. Separating them is one of the simplest changes you can make to train better and gain range over time.

## Mobility isn't flexibility

- **Mobility** is actively controlling a joint through a range: rotating your hips, raising your arms overhead without arching your lower back. You train it with movement.
- **Flexibility** is reaching bigger ranges and holding them: the front split, the pike, the bridge. You train it with time under stretch.

## Before lifting: dynamic activation

Ten to fifteen minutes of controlled movement, chosen for what you're about to train:

- **Back day:** cat-cow, quadruped thoracic rotations, wall slides, scapular pull-ups and dead hangs.
- **Leg day:** ankle rocks, leg swings, 90/90 hip switches, a deep squat with movement and walking lunges.
- **Chest and shoulder day:** wrist and shoulder circles, scapular push-ups and banded external rotations.

Don't spend ten minutes in a deep split before deadlifting: long static holds before heavy loads don't prepare your body for the work.

## After lifting: static stretching

This is where held positions belong, 30 to 60 seconds at moderate tension and pain-free. It's the time for the couch stretch, pigeon pose, half split or puppy pose, depending on the muscles you trained.

## Active strength: what makes the difference

Splits and handstands don't improve with passive stretching alone. Add strength work in those positions: active leg raises for the front split, Cossack squats for the middle split, hollow holds and pike compressions for the handstand.
